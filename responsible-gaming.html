Responsible Gaming Guide

Important: Gaming should never put your financial security, health, education, work, or personal relationships at risk.

1. What Is Responsible Gaming?

Responsible gaming means making informed decisions about participation in games, setting appropriate boundaries, understanding risks, and knowing when to stop.

Some online games involve paid participation or the possibility of losing money. Such activities require additional caution.

2. Understand Financial Risks

Games involving real money can lead to financial loss. Results may be uncertain, and past outcomes do not guarantee future success.

Never treat gaming as a reliable income source or a solution to financial problems.

3. Set Spending Limits

If you choose to participate in legally permitted paid gaming, establish a strict entertainment budget that you can afford to lose without affecting essential expenses.

  • Never use money needed for rent, food, bills, or education.
  • Do not borrow money to participate.
  • Do not increase spending to recover previous losses.
  • Stop immediately when your predetermined limit is reached.

4. Manage Your Time

Gaming should not interfere with sleep, work, studies, family responsibilities, or physical well-being.

Set time limits and take regular breaks from screens.

5. Never Chase Losses

Trying to recover losses by playing longer or spending more can increase financial harm. Accept that losses are possible and avoid making decisions under pressure.

6. Age Restrictions

Minors should not participate in age-restricted or real-money gaming activities.

Adults must check applicable age requirements and local laws before accessing any gaming platform.

7. Recognize Warning Signs

Possible warning signs of harmful gaming behavior include:

  • Spending more money or time than intended
  • Hiding gaming activity from family members
  • Borrowing money to continue playing
  • Feeling anxious or distressed when unable to play
  • Neglecting work, education, or relationships
  • Repeated attempts to recover losses

8. Take a Break or Stop

If gaming becomes difficult to control, consider stopping participation, removing related applications, disabling notifications, and using account restriction or self-exclusion tools where available.

9. Seek Support

If gaming is affecting your finances, mental health, or relationships, speak with someone you trust and consider support from a qualified mental health professional or a specialist service for gambling-related harm.

If you feel unsafe or are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

10. Account and Payment Security

Protect your personal information, avoid sharing OTPs or passwords, and carefully review third-party websites before providing financial details.
